Committees

United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP)

Topic 1: Global Carbon Emission Reductions
Theme: Achieving net-zero carbon emissions by 2050.
Discussion: What strategies can countries adopt to transition to low-carbon economies and achieve their nationally determined contributions (NDCs) under the Paris Agreement? How can international cooperation ensure equitable distribution of climate action responsibilities between developed and developing nations?

Topic 2: Financing Climate Action
Theme: Mobilizing climate finance for mitigation and adaptation projects.
Discussion: How can developed countries fulfill their climate finance commitments to provide $100 billion annually to support climate projects in developing nations? What role can private investments and green bonds play in financing large-scale climate initiatives?

United Nations Human Rights Council (UNHRC)

Topic 1: Protecting the Rights of Refugees and Migrants
Theme: Safeguarding the human rights of refugees and migrants, reducing discrimination, and promoting social inclusion.
Discussion: How can countries better uphold international refugee laws and integrate migrants into society while reducing xenophobia and discrimination?

Topic 2: Protecting individuals' digital rights and privacy in the age of technology and surveillance.
Theme: Protecting individuals' digital rights and privacy in the age of technology and surveillance
Discussion: How should human rights evolve in the digital age, especially concerning government surveillance, data collection, and cyber privacy?

Disarmament and International Security Committee (DISEC)

Topic 1: Disarmament for Sustainable Peace
Theme: Enhancing nuclear non-proliferation measures to promote international peace.
Discussion: Reducing small arms and light weapons trafficking and its role in fostering violence in unstable regions. Promoting nuclear disarmament to minimize the risk of escalation and warfare. Enhancing international cooperation on demilitarization to foster global peace.

Topic 2: Nuclear Non-Proliferation and Energy Security
Theme: Stopping nuclear arms and advancing nuclear power.
Discussion: Balancing nuclear non-proliferation with countries’ right to develop nuclear energy for peaceful purposes. Strengthening international safeguards to ensure nuclear energy is used responsibly. Discussing alternative energy sources to reduce the dependency on nuclear programs for energy needs.

DISEC Background Guide

United Nations Development Programme (UNDP)

Topic 1: Automation, AI, and the Future of Work
Theme: Preparing the workforce for the impact of automation and artificial intelligence.
Discussion: How will automation and AI impact jobs in the future, particularly in industries such as manufacturing and services? What measures should be taken to reskill and upskill workers to prepare them for the jobs of the future?

Topic 2: Women’s Economic Empowerment
Theme: Promoting gender equality in the workplace and supporting women’s economic participation.
Discussion: What policies can countries implement to promote women’s participation in the labor force, particularly in sectors where they are underrepresented? How can barriers to women’s economic empowerment, such as wage gaps and discrimination, be addressed?

UNDP Background Guide

United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO)

Topic 1: Education in Conflict Zones
Theme: Providing access to education for children in conflict and post-conflict areas.
Discussion: What measures can be taken to ensure children in conflict zones or refugee camps have access to education? How can UNESCO and the international community support the rebuilding of education systems in post-conflict areas?

Topic 2: Access to Quality Education for All
Theme: Bridging the gap in access to education between urban and rural areas.
Discussion: How can countries ensure equal access to quality education for children in remote or underprivileged areas? What role can technology and digital tools play in providing educational opportunities to those who lack access to traditional schools?

International Press Corps (IPC)

The International Press Corps is unlike any other committee. It is a multifaceted committee and requires determined individuals with a passion for truth and a deep familiarity with journalism. Here, participants do not represent nations but rather international news organisations. Regular MUN protocol is not followed, and participants are called “journalists” or "reporters," not delegates. Reporters will be assigned to certain committees and tasked with covering developments within the committees, organising and attending press conferences, writing articles, and interviewing delegates, all with precision and objectivity. Since the IPC sharpens the public speaking, writing, and debate skills of participants, it is also arguably the most holistic committee. However, don’t let this intimidate you! The key to succeeding in IPC is prior research and thorough preparation. As the age-old saying goes, "The pen is mightier than the sword." Avoid inaccurate facts and fallacious arguments. Most importantly, be vocal - for words carry power in the IPC.
